# Competitor and Evidence Research — 22 August 2026

## Initial findings

| Source | Verified capability or evidence | Implication for Adaptly |
|---|---|---|
| Brisk Teaching | Brisk positions itself inside teachers’ existing Google/Microsoft workflows and promotes resource creation, personalised feedback, standards-aligned next steps, batch feedback, writing-process inspection, reading-level transformation and translation. | Adaptly must make trusted curriculum/SEND adaptations usable at the point of work, connect worksheet evidence to next steps, and reduce teacher switching costs. |
| MagicSchool | MagicSchool markets 80+ teacher tools, student learning insights, district dashboards, safety controls, professional learning, and integrations with Google Docs, Classroom and Canvas. | Adaptly needs a coherent teacher workspace, practical insight loop, school-level governance and safe configurable AI—not isolated tool pages. |
| Education Endowment Foundation SEND guidance | The EEF describes five broadly supportive approaches for pupils with SEND: explicit instruction, cognitive/metacognitive strategies, scaffolding, flexible grouping and technology. It emphasises temporary, targeted scaffolding and ongoing formative assessment. | Differentiation should be visible, evidence-informed and removable over time. Product priorities should include guided-to-independent worksheet modes, formative diagnostic capture, flexible pupil groups, and teacher-facing adaptation rationale. |

## UK trust and governance findings

| Source | Verified guidance | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Department for Education | AI outputs may be inaccurate, biased, unsafe or insufficiently curriculum-specific; the professional remains responsible for checking final output. It identifies teacher-facing resource creation, tailored feedback and administrative support as potential uses, while stressing safety, reliability, supervision and transparency. | Every AI-generated worksheet, schedule decision and auto-mark must identify its source, support a teacher review/override pathway and retain enough provenance for an accountable human decision. |
| Department for Education data-protection guidance | Schools should plan AI use carefully, be transparent about personal-data processing, use approved tools, fact-check outputs and avoid entering identifiable pupil data unless the data is protected and not used to train models. | Build a data-minimised generation boundary, a clear in-product AI-use statement, safe display names/initials where practical, explicit teacher approval for high-consequence decisions and an audit trail. |
| Information Commissioner’s Office | Edtech providers may fall within the Children’s Code where children are likely to access the service; high privacy by default and the child’s best interests are relevant good practice. | Pupil-facing scheduler and history views should default to the least data and least intrusive presentation, avoid dark patterns, clearly distinguish teacher-only content and retain privacy-first settings. |
