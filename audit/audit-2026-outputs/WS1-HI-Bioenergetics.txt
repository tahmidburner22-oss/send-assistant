By the end of this lesson, students will be able to explain the processes of photosynthesis and respiration, and their interdependence in bioenergetics.

Photosynthesis:
The process by which green plants and some other organisms use sunlight to synthesise foods with the help of chlorophyll pigment.nRespiration — The process by which organisms combine oxygen with food molecules, diverting the energy in these substances into life-sustaining activities and releasing carbon dioxide and water.nChlorophyll — The green pigment found in chloroplasts that absorbs light energy for photosynthesis.nATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) — The molecule that stores and releases energy for use by cells.nMitochondria — The organelles in eukaryotic cells that generate most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), used as a source of chemical energy.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING PHOTOSYNTHESIS AND RESPIRATION. → PHOTOSYNTHESIS USES LIGHT ENERGY TO MAKE GLUCOSE AND OXYGEN FROM CARBON DIOXIDE AND WATER. RESPIRATION BREAKS DOWN GLUCOSE USING OXYGEN TO RELEASE ENERGY, PRODUCING CARBON DIOXIDE AND WATER.
MISTAKE 2: THINKING RESPIRATION ONLY HAPPENS IN ANIMALS. → RESPIRATION OCCURS IN ALL LIVING ORGANISMS, INCLUDING PLANTS, FUNGI, AND BACTERIA. PLANTS RESPIRE ALL THE TIME, BUT PHOTOSYNTHESISE ONLY WHEN LIGHT IS AVAILABLE.
MISTAKE 3: NOT UNDERSTANDING THE ROLE OF ATP. → ATP IS THE IMMEDIATE ENERGY CURRENCY OF THE CELL. IT IS NOT THE SAME AS GLUCOSE; GLUCOSE IS A STORAGE MOLECULE, WHILE ATP IS USED FOR IMMEDIATE ENERGY RELEASE FOR CELLULAR PROCESSES.
WORKED EXAMPLE
— Study this example carefully.
1
nQuestion: Calculate the energy released from 1 mole of glucose during aerobic respiration.
nStep 1: Identify the relevant process: Aerobic respiration breaks down glucose to release energy.nStep 2: Recall the standard energy release per mole of glucose: Aerobic respiration releases approximately 3000 kJ of energy per mole of glucose.nStep 3: State the answer with units: The energy released is 3000 kJ/mol. ✓ Key point: Aerobic respiration is a highly efficient process for releasing energy stored in glucose.
