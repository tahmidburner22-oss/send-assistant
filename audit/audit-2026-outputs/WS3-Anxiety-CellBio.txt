CLASS

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain the stages of the cell cycle and describe how mitosis makes two identical daughter cells.

Chromosome:
A thread-like structure inside the nucleus made of DNA and protein. It carries genetic information.
Mitosis:
A type of cell division that creates two daughter cells. They have the same number and type of chromosomes as the parent cell.
Cell Cycle:
The stages a cell goes through as it grows and divides to make two new cells.
DNA Replication:
The process where a DNA molecule is copied to make two identical DNA molecules.
Cytokinesis:
The physical splitting of a parent cell into two daughter cells. It divides the cytoplasm.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING MITOSIS WITH MEIOSIS → MITOSIS MAKES IDENTICAL BODY CELLS FOR GROWTH AND REPAIR. MEIOSIS MAKES DIFFERENT GAMETES FOR SEXUAL REPRODUCTION.
MISTAKE 2: THINKING DNA IS ONLY COPIED DURING MITOSIS → DNA REPLICATION HAPPENS IN THE INTERPHASE STAGE BEFORE MITOSIS STARTS.
MISTAKE 3: MISUNDERSTANDING THE NUMBER OF CHROMOSOMES → THE DAUGHTER CELLS FROM MITOSIS HAVE THE SAME NUMBER OF CHROMOSOMES AS THE PARENT CELL, NOT HALF.
WORKED EXAMPLE
— Study this example carefully. Scenario: A human skin cell with 46 chromosomes undergoes mitosis. Step 1: Interphase — The cell grows and copies its DNA. Each chromosome now has two copies.
1
Mitosis — The chromosomes line up in the middle of the cell. They are then pulled to opposite ends.
2
Cytokinesis — The cytoplasm divides. This results in two daughter cells. Each has 46 chromosomes. ✓ Key point: Mitosis ensures each new cell gets an exact copy of the parent cell's genetic material.
