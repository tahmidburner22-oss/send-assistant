By the end of this lesson, students will be able to explain the structure of atoms, the arrangement of electrons in shells, and the relationship between atomic structure and the position of elements in the periodic table.

Atom:
The basic unit of a chemical element.nProton — A stable subatomic particle with a positive electric charge.nNeutron — A subatomic particle with no net electric charge.nElectron — A stable subatomic particle with a negative electric charge.nAtomic Number — The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom, which determines its chemical properties.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING ATOMIC NUMBER AND MASS NUMBER. → THE ATOMIC NUMBER IS THE NUMBER OF PROTONS ONLY. THE MASS NUMBER IS THE TOTAL NUMBER OF PROTONS AND NEUTRONS IN THE NUCLEUS.
MISTAKE 2: INCORRECTLY FILLING ELECTRON SHELLS. → ELECTRONS FILL SHELLS IN A SPECIFIC ORDER: 2 IN THE FIRST SHELL, 8 IN THE SECOND, AND 8 IN THE THIRD (FOR THE FIRST 20 ELEMENTS). DO NOT FILL THE SECOND SHELL BEFORE THE FIRST IS FULL.
MISTAKE 3: ASSUMING ALL ATOMS OF AN ELEMENT HAVE THE SAME MASS. → ISOTOPES ARE ATOMS OF THE SAME ELEMENT WITH DIFFERENT NUMBERS OF NEUTRONS, AND THEREFORE DIFFERENT MASS NUMBERS. FOR EXAMPLE, CARBON-12 AND CARBON-14 ARE ISOTOPES OF CARBON.
WORKED EXAMPLE
— Study this example carefully.
1
nCalculate the number of neutrons in an atom of Magnesium-25.
nStep 1: Identify the atomic number and mass number from the periodic table or given information. Magnesium (Mg) has an atomic number of 12 and a mass number of 25.nStep 2: Recall the relationship: Mass Number = Number of Protons + Number of Neutrons.nStep 3: Rearrange the formula to find the number of neutrons: Number of Neutrons = Mass Number - Number of Protons. Substitute the values: Number of Neutrons = 25 − 12 = 13. ✓ Key point: The atomic number always equals the number of protons, which is constant for a given element.
