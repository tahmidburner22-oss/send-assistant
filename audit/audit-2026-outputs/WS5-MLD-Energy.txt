By the end of this lesson, students will be able to describe different energy stores and transfers, and apply the principle of conservation of energy.

Energy:
the ability to do work or cause change.nStore — a place where energy is kept.nTransfer — to move energy from one store to another.nWork — when a force moves an object over a distance.nConservation of energy — the principle that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or changed from one form to another.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: THINKING ENERGY IS 'USED UP'. → ENERGY IS CONSERVED. IT IS TRANSFERRED FROM ONE STORE TO ANOTHER, OR TRANSFORMED INTO DIFFERENT FORMS, BUT THE TOTAL AMOUNT OF ENERGY STAYS THE SAME.
MISTAKE 2: CONFUSING MASS AND WEIGHT. → MASS IS THE AMOUNT OF 'STUFF' IN AN OBJECT (MEASURED IN KG). WEIGHT IS THE FORCE OF GRAVITY ON AN OBJECT (MEASURED IN N).
MISTAKE 3: FORGETTING TO INCLUDE UNITS. → ALWAYS WRITE THE CORRECT UNIT AFTER YOUR ANSWER (E.G., JOULES (J) FOR ENERGY, NEWTONS (N) FOR FORCE).
WORKED EXAMPLE
— Study this example carefully.
1
nA student pushes a box across the floor with a force of 50 N. The box moves 2 metres.
nStep 1: Identify the force and distance: Force = 50 N, Distance = 2 m.nStep 2: Recall the formula for work done: Work done = Force × distance.nStep 3: Calculate the work done: Work done = 50 N × 2 m = 100 J.nStep 4: State the answer with units: The work done is 100 J. ✓ Key point: Work is done when a force causes movement, and it represents a transfer of energy.
