CLASS

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to identify and describe different types of forces and their effects on objects.

Force:
A push or pull on an object.nMass — The amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg).nWeight — The force of gravity acting on an object, measured in Newtons (N).nFriction — A force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact.nGravity — The force of attraction between any two objects with mass.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING MASS AND WEIGHT. → MASS IS THE AMOUNT OF 'STUFF' IN AN OBJECT (MEASURED IN KG), WHILE WEIGHT IS THE FORCE OF GRAVITY PULLING ON THAT MASS (MEASURED IN N). WEIGHT CHANGES WITH GRAVITY, MASS DOES NOT.
MISTAKE 2: THINKING FRICTION ALWAYS STOPS THINGS. → FRICTION IS A FORCE THAT OPPOSES MOTION. IT CAN BE USEFUL (E.G. BRAKES) OR UNHELPFUL (E.G. AIR RESISTANCE). IT ACTS IN THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION TO MOVEMENT.
MISTAKE 3: ASSUMING FORCES ARE ALWAYS BALANCED. → IF AN OBJECT IS ACCELERATING (SPEEDING UP, SLOWING DOWN, OR CHANGING DIRECTION), THE FORCES ACTING ON IT ARE UNBALANCED. BALANCED FORCES MEAN CONSTANT SPEED OR STATIONARY.
