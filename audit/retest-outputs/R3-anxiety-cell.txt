CLASS

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to describe the structure and function of specialised cells and explain how they are adapted for their specific roles.

Differentiation:
The process by which a cell becomes specialised for a specific function.nEukaryotic — Cells that contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.nProkaryotic — Cells that do not contain a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.nSpecialised — Cells that have developed specific features to perform a particular job.nOrganelle — A specialised structure within a cell that performs a specific function.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING ANIMAL AND PLANT CELLS. → REMEMBER THAT ONLY PLANT CELLS HAVE A CELL WALL AND CHLOROPLASTS; ANIMAL CELLS DO NOT.
MISTAKE 2: THINKING ALL CELLS ARE THE SAME. → CELLS DIFFERENTIATE TO BECOME SPECIALISED; THEY ARE NOT ALL IDENTICAL IN STRUCTURE.
MISTAKE 3: MISUNDERSTANDING THE ROLE OF THE NUCLEUS. → THE NUCLEUS CONTAINS GENETIC MATERIAL (DNA), IT IS NOT THE SITE OF ENERGY PRODUCTION.
