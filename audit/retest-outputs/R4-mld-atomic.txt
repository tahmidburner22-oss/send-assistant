Atom:
the smallest part of an element that can exist and still be that elementnProton — a particle in the nucleus of an atom with a positive chargenNeutron — a particle in the nucleus of an atom with no electrical chargenElectron — a particle with a negative charge that orbits the nucleusnAtomic number — the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom of an element
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING MASS WITH WEIGHT → MASS IS THE AMOUNT OF MATTER IN AN OBJECT MEASURED IN KILOGRAMS (KG), BUT WEIGHT IS A FORCE MEASURED IN NEWTONS (N) THAT DEPENDS ON GRAVITY.
MISTAKE 2: MIXING UP ATOMS, ELEMENTS, AND COMPOUNDS → AN ELEMENT CONTAINS ONE TYPE OF ATOM ONLY, BUT A COMPOUND CONTAINS ATOMS OF TWO OR MORE ELEMENTS CHEMICALLY JOINED. ATOMS ARE THE BUILDING BLOCKS.
MISTAKE 3: THINKING ELECTRONS ARE IN FIXED POSITIONS → ELECTRONS MOVE IN SHELLS (ENERGY LEVELS) AROUND THE NUCLEUS, NOT IN FIXED PLACES; SHELLS FILL IN A SPECIFIC ORDER.
WORKED EXAMPLE
— Study this example carefully.
1
nCalculate the number of neutrons in an atom of chlorine if the atomic number is 17 and the mass number is 35.
nStep 1: Identify given values: Atomic number = 17, Mass number = 35nStep 2: Use the formula: Number of neutrons = Mass number - Atomic numbernStep 3: Substitute values: Neutrons = 35 − 17nStep 4: Calculate: Neutrons = 18 ✓ Key point: Neutrons equal the mass number minus the atomic number.
