By the end of this lesson, students will be able to explain the processes of aerobic and anaerobic respiration and their importance in releasing energy from glucose.

Respiration:
The process of transferring energy from glucose which goes on in every cell.nAerobic — A type of respiration that uses oxygen to release energy from glucose.nAnaerobic — A type of respiration that occurs without oxygen, releasing less energy.nMetabolism — The sum of all the chemical reactions that happen in a cell or the body.nATP — The molecule that stores the energy released during respiration for cellular processes.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING BREATHING WITH RESPIRATION. → RESPIRATION IS A CHEMICAL REACTION IN CELLS, NOT THE PHYSICAL ACT OF BREATHING (VENTILATION).
MISTAKE 2: THINKING ANAEROBIC RESPIRATION PRODUCES NO ENERGY. → IT PRODUCES ENERGY, BUT SIGNIFICANTLY LESS THAN AEROBIC RESPIRATION BECAUSE GLUCOSE IS NOT FULLY OXIDISED.
MISTAKE 3: ASSUMING LACTIC ACID IS PRODUCED IN PLANTS. → PLANTS AND YEAST PRODUCE ETHANOL AND CO₂ DURING ANAEROBIC RESPIRATION, NOT LACTIC ACID.
WORKED EXAMPLE
— Study this example carefully.
1
nQuestion: Calculate the total energy released if 2 moles of glucose are fully oxidised in aerobic respiration, given that 1 mole of glucose releases 2800 kJ of energy.
nStep 1: Identify the formula: Total Energy = Moles of Glucose × Energy per mole.nStep 2: Substitute the values: Total Energy = 2 mol × 2800 kJ/mol.nStep 3: Calculate the result: 5600 kJ. ✓ Key point: Always ensure units are consistent before performing the final calculation.
