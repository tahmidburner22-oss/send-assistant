## Curriculum sources and implications

The statutory DfE science programme describes KS1 as centred on first-hand observation, curiosity, simple scientific enquiry, and increasingly precise basic vocabulary. The programme lists Year 1 content in plants, animals including humans, everyday materials, and seasonal changes, with explicit opportunities to draw plant parts and classify animals. The library therefore needs visually engaging, low-text, high-contrast and object-centred KS1 assets that support observation, sorting and labelling—not mini GCSE diagrams. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study

The AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy specification organises assessed science through distinct biology, chemistry and physics subject-content sections and specifies 21 required practical activities. Its biology content page explicitly identifies content sections, working scientifically, mathematical requirements, apparatus and required practicals. The image metadata model must consequently carry a discipline, qualification level, foundation/higher suitability, specification/reference tags, required-practical flag, and a precise subtopic key. Source: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/science/gcse/science-8464/specification and https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/science/gcse/science-8464/specification/biology-subject-content

The DfE KS3 programme specifies the conceptual building blocks to model accurately: cell structure and microscopy; tissues to organ systems; skeleton/muscles; digestion; gas exchange; reproduction; photosynthesis and respiration; ecosystems; inheritance, variation and natural selection; particle model; separation techniques; reaction equations; periodic table; energy; forces; waves; electricity; magnetism; matter; and space. Visual assets at this level need precise scientific notation, correctly scaled/ordered causal relationships, labelled variables and SI units where applicable.

The official AQA chemistry and physics content pages confirm the same specification-level structure. Chemistry includes the periodic table, bonding/structure/properties, quantitative chemistry, chemical changes, energy changes, rates/equilibrium, organic chemistry, chemical analysis, atmosphere and resources. Physics includes energy, electricity, particle model, atomic structure, forces, waves, magnetism/electromagnetism and space physics. Their generation profiles must reject invalid particle counts/formulae, circuit symbols, ray arrows, energy pathways, units and graphical axes before assets can be published. Sources: https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/science/gcse/science-8464/specification/chemistry-subject-content and https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/science/gcse/science-8464/specification/physics-subject-content

## First GPT Image 2 validation set

Five cross-stage anchor diagrams were generated with GPT Image 2 (high quality) to test the production prompt framework. The KS1 flowering-plant image and KS2 water-cycle image are visually clear and scientifically suitable for primary use, subject to routine editorial approval. The GCSE sodium-chloride ionic-bonding diagram is broadly faithful to the requested electron transfer and charge conventions, but remains pending detailed chemistry review before publication. The KS3 plant/animal-cell image must be rejected because the leader line for `Nucleus` terminates at a chloroplast rather than the nucleus. The GCSE circuit comparison must be rejected because the purported series circuit has an open switch despite current arrows and the requested closed circuit. This confirms the need for a mandatory human scientific-review state rather than automatic publishing of generated diagrams.
