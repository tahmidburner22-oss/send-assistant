By the end of this lesson, students will be able to explain the principles of electromagnetism and apply them to solve problems involving circuits and magnetic fields.

Electromagnetism:
the interaction between electric currents or fields and magnetic fields.nCurrent — the rate of flow of electric charge, measured in amperes (A).nMagnetic field — a region around a magnetic material or a moving electric charge within which the force of magnetism acts.nResistance — the opposition to the flow of electric current in a circuit, measured in ohms (Ω).nVoltage — the electric potential difference between two points, measured in volts (V).
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING CURRENT AND VOLTAGE. → VOLTAGE IS THE 'PUSH' THAT MAKES CURRENT FLOW. CURRENT IS THE ACTUAL FLOW OF CHARGE. THEY ARE NOT THE SAME THING.
MISTAKE 2: THINKING CURRENT IS 'USED UP' IN A CIRCUIT. → IN A SERIES CIRCUIT, THE CURRENT IS THE SAME EVERYWHERE. IT IS THE ENERGY CARRIED BY THE CURRENT THAT IS TRANSFERRED TO COMPONENTS LIKE BULBS OR RESISTORS.
MISTAKE 3: FORGETTING TO INCLUDE UNITS IN CALCULATIONS. → ALWAYS STATE THE CORRECT SI UNIT WITH YOUR FINAL ANSWER. FOR EXAMPLE, RESISTANCE IS MEASURED IN OHMS (Ω), NOT JUST A NUMBER.
