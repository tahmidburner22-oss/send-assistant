By the end of this lesson, students will be able to identify and describe the basic structure of an atom, including protons, neutrons, and electrons, and understand their relative positions and charges.

Atom:
The basic unit of a chemical element.nProton — A positively charged particle found in the nucleus of an atom.nNeutron — A particle with no electric charge found in the nucleus of an atom.nElectron — A negatively charged particle that orbits the nucleus of an atom.nNucleus — The central part of an atom, containing protons and neutrons.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING THE NUCLEUS WITH THE WHOLE ATOM. → THE NUCLEUS IS ONLY THE CENTRE PART OF THE ATOM; IT CONTAINS PROTONS AND NEUTRONS, BUT ELECTRONS ORBIT OUTSIDE IT.
MISTAKE 2: THINKING ELECTRONS HAVE NO MASS. → ELECTRONS HAVE A VERY SMALL MASS, ALTHOUGH IT IS MUCH SMALLER THAN THE MASS OF PROTONS OR NEUTRONS.
MISTAKE 3: INCORRECTLY ASSIGNING CHARGES TO SUBATOMIC PARTICLES. → PROTONS ARE POSITIVE (+1), NEUTRONS ARE NEUTRAL (0), AND ELECTRONS ARE NEGATIVE (-1).
WORKED EXAMPLE
— Study this example carefully.
1
nAn atom of Lithium (Li) has 3 protons and 4 neutrons in its nucleus. How many electrons does it have?
nStep 1: Identify the number of protons. Lithium has 3 protons.nStep 2: Understand that a neutral atom has an equal number of protons and electrons.nStep 3: Therefore, a neutral Lithium atom has 3 electrons. ✓ Key point: In a neutral atom, the number of positive protons equals the number of negative electrons.
