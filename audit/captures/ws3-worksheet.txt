LEARNING OBJECTIVE

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to describe the structure of an atom, including the subatomic particles and their relative charges and masses.

KEY VOCABULARY
Atom:
The basic unit of a chemical element.
Proton:
A positively charged particle found in the nucleus of an atom.
Neutron:
A particle with no charge found in the nucleus of an atom.
Electron:
A negatively charged particle that orbits the nucleus of an atom.
Nucleus:
The central core of an atom, containing protons and neutrons.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
Watch out for these common errors:
Mistake 1: Confusing mass number and atomic number. → The atomic number is the number of protons. The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.
Mistake 2: Thinking electrons have significant mass. → Electrons have a very small mass compared to protons and neutrons, often considered negligible in mass number calculations.
Mistake 3: Incorrectly assigning charges to subatomic particles. → Protons are positive (+1), neutrons are neutral (0), and electrons are negative (-1).
WORKED EXAMPLE
Study this example carefully.
1
An atom has 6 protons and 6 neutrons.
2
Identify the number of protons. This is given as 6.
3
Identify the number of neutrons. This is given as 6.
4
Calculate the mass number by adding the number of protons and neutrons: 6 + 6 = 12.
5
State the atomic number, which is equal to the number of protons: 6.
6
Write the symbol for the element if known, or describe the atom's structure. For example, Carbon has 6 protons. ✓ Key point: The atomic number defines the element, while the mass number indicates the isotope.
