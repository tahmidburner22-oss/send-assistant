LEARNING OBJECTIVE

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain the structure and function of different cell types and their organisation into tissues, organs, and organ systems.

KEY VOCABULARY
Cell:
The basic structural and functional unit of all known living organisms.
Tissue:
A group of similar cells that perform a specific function.
Organ:
A structure made up of a group of tissues that work together to perform a specific function.
Organelle:
A specialised subunit within a cell that has a specific function.
Eukaryotic cell:
A cell that contains a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
Watch out for these common errors:
Mistake 1: Confusing cell parts with whole cells. → Remember that organelles like the nucleus or mitochondria are parts within a cell, not the cell itself.
Mistake 2: Assuming all cells are the same. → Different cell types (e.g., nerve cells, root hair cells) have specialised structures that relate directly to their specific jobs.
Mistake 3: Misunderstanding the hierarchy of organisation. → Cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs form organ systems. Do not reverse this order.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Study this example carefully. Question: Calculate the magnification of a microscope image of a mitochondrion that is 50 mm long, when the actual mitochondrion is 25 µm long.
1
Ensure units are consistent. Convert 25 µm to mm: 25 µm = 0.025 mm.
2
Use the formula: Magnification = Image size / Actual size.
3
Substitute values: Magnification = 50 mm / 0.025 mm.
4
Calculate the result: Magnification = 2000. ✓ Key point: Always convert units to be the same before calculating magnification.
