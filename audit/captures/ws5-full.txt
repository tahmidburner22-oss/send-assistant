LEARNING OBJECTIVE

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to analyse how Shakespeare uses language and structure to present characters and themes in Macbeth and Romeo and Juliet.

KEY VOCABULARY
Tragedy:
A play dealing with tragic events and having an unhappy ending, especially one concerning the downfall of the main character.
Soliloquy:
An act of speaking one's thoughts aloud when by oneself or regardless of any hearers, especially by a character in a play.
Aside:
A short speech or comment made by an actor in a play, which is supposed to be heard by the audience but not by other characters on the stage.
Metaphor:
A figure of speech in which a word or phrase is applied to an object or action to which it is not literally applicable, without using 'like' or 'as'.
Iambic Pentameter:
A line of verse with five metrical feet, each consisting of one short (or unstressed) syllable followed by one long (or stressed) syllable.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
Watch out for these common errors:
Mistake 1: Confusing simile and metaphor. → A simile uses 'like' or 'as' to compare two things (e.g., 'as brave as a lion'). A metaphor states that one thing IS another (e.g., 'he is a lion').
Mistake 2: Only identifying literary devices without explaining their effect. → Simply naming a technique (like 'personification') is not enough. You must explain how it affects the audience or what it reveals about a character or theme.
Mistake 3: Summarising the plot instead of analysing the writer's methods. → The focus should be on how Shakespeare writes, not just what happens in the story. Analyse word choice, sentence structure, and imagery.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Study this example carefully.
1
Analyse how Shakespeare presents Lady Macbeth's ambition in this extract: Is this a dagger which I see before me, The handle toward my hand? Come, let me clutch thee! I have thee not, and yet I see thee still. Art thou not, fatal vision, sensible To feeling as to sight? or art thou but A dagger of the mind, a false creation, Proceeding from the heat-oppressed brain?
2
Identify the key character and the theme being presented (Lady Macbeth's ambition).
3
Select relevant quotations and identify Shakespeare's methods. For example, the 'dagger' could be a metaphor for the bloody deed she is contemplating, driven by ambition.
4
Explain the effect of these methods. The 'heat-oppressed brain' suggests her ambition is consuming her thoughts, making her hallucinate, showing the psychological toll. ✓ Key point: Focus on how the language and imagery reveal the character's inner state and motivations.
