Newton's Second Law:
The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.
Resultant force:
The overall force acting on an object when all individual forces are combined.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
Watch out for these common errors:
Mistake 1: Confusing mass and weight. → Mass is the amount of 'stuff' in an object (measured in kg), while weight is the force of gravity acting on that mass (measured in N). They are not the same.
Mistake 2: Thinking force and acceleration are always proportional. → While force causes acceleration, the acceleration also depends on the mass. A larger mass requires a larger force to achieve the same acceleration.
Mistake 3: Assuming a moving object has no forces acting on it. → An object can move at a constant velocity if the resultant force is zero (e.g. balanced forces). Forces are still acting, but they cancel each other out.
