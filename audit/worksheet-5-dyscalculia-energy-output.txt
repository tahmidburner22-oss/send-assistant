By the end of this lesson, students will be able to analyse and evaluate different energy transfer pathways and resource sustainability.

Energy store:
The energy a system possesses due to its state or position.nEnergy transfer — The movement of energy from one store to another.nConservation of energy — Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or changed from one form to another.nRenewable resource — A resource that can be replenished naturally over a short period.nNon-renewable resource — A resource that exists in finite quantities and is consumed much faster than it can be formed.
Watch out for these common errors:
MISTAKE 1: CONFUSING WEIGHT AND MASS. → MASS IS THE AMOUNT OF MATTER IN AN OBJECT (MEASURED IN KG), WHILE WEIGHT IS THE FORCE OF GRAVITY ACTING ON THAT MASS (MEASURED IN N). THEY ARE NOT THE SAME.
MISTAKE 2: THINKING ENERGY IS 'USED UP'. → ENERGY IS CONSERVED; IT IS TRANSFERRED OR TRANSFORMED, NOT DESTROYED. WHEN WORK IS DONE, ENERGY MOVES BETWEEN STORES.
MISTAKE 3: ASSUMING PRIMARY SOURCES ARE ALWAYS MORE RELIABLE. → RELIABILITY DEPENDS ON THE SOURCE'S PURPOSE, BIAS, AND ACCURACY, NOT JUST WHETHER IT'S PRIMARY OR SECONDARY. SECONDARY SOURCES CAN OFFER VALUABLE ANALYSIS.
