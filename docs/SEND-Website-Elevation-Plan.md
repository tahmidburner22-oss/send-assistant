# SEND Website Elevation Plan
## Competitor Research & SEND-Adapted Strategy

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This plan takes the best ideas from **seven** leading education platforms and reimagines them specifically for **SEND (Special Educational Needs & Disabilities)** learners and the teachers/TAs who support them. Your USP is that NO other platform does what these sites do **with SEND learners at the centre**. Every tool, every feature, every interaction is designed with accessibility, differentiation, scaffolding, and sensory needs built in from the ground up — not bolted on as an afterthought.

## PART 1: COMPETITOR ANALYSIS — WHAT MAKES THEM GREAT

### 1. EasyClass.ai — The "Connected OS"
**What they do brilliantly:**
- One input (a topic/lesson plan) generates an ENTIRE connected instructional set
- Outputs: worksheet + presentation slides + reading passage + quiz + grading rubric
- Everything is aligned and organised in one unified workspace
- Teachers stop bouncing between apps — everything lives in one place
- Saves 5-10 hours per week

**Why it works:** Teachers are burned out. They want ONE place where they type a topic and get everything they need. The "connected" part is key — the worksheet references the same content as the slides and the quiz.

---

### 2. BuildMyStory.com — The "Published Author" Reading Platform
**What they do brilliantly:**
- Every child becomes a published author of their own storybook
- Structured process: teacher guides brainstorming → writing on paper → building on website → published e-book → optional printed book
- Provided resources for each project: lesson plans, slides, worksheets
- Free e-book for every student; families can buy printed copies (~$19.95)
- The MOTIVATION factor: knowing your work becomes a REAL book changes how kids engage with writing
- Celebration moment: class events where children hold their published book

**Why it works:** The output is tangible and celebratory. Kids push through the hard parts of writing because they know it ends with something real. It's not "do this worksheet" — it's "write YOUR book."

---

### 3. ToolsEdu.com — Interactive Learning Games in Seconds
**What they do brilliantly:**
- AI generates themed puzzles from any topic or vocabulary list
- Tools include: quizzes, word searches, crosswords, themed puzzles
- Instant generation — "lesson prep in seconds"
- Focus on making learning interactive and game-like
- Students engage with content through play rather than passive worksheets

**Why it works:** Gamification works. Students who won't engage with a traditional worksheet WILL engage with a word search or crossword on the same content. The AI removes the creation burden from teachers.

---

### 4. MyLens.ai — AI Visual Thinking Platform
**What they do brilliantly:**
- Turns ANY input (text, documents, videos, URLs) into interactive visuals
- Visual types: timelines, mind maps, flowcharts, tables, cheatsheets, SWOT boards
- AI auto-selects the best visual format for the content
- Fully interactive: click to expand, drill down into detail, explore connections
- Source citation tracking — every insight traced back to original source
- For teachers: "spark curiosity and make lessons more interactive"
- For students: "learn faster and understand concepts in depth"

**Why it works:** Visual learning is powerful for ALL learners, but especially for those who struggle with text-heavy content. The interactive element means students can explore at their own pace and depth.

---

### 5. Gamma.app — AI-Native Presentation Platform
**What they do brilliantly:**
- Built from the ground up for AI-generated presentations (not PowerPoint + AI bolt-on)
- "Text-to-design" workflow: paste notes, a prompt, outline, or upload a document → complete presentation in 30-60 seconds
- Uses 20+ AI models working together for text, imagery, layout, and design consistency
- Automatically handles layout composition, typography, spacing, and visual consistency
- Web-native delivery: presentations are live shareable links (not static downloaded files)
- Card-based scrollable layouts — modern alternative to traditional 16:9 slides
- Import anything: documents, URLs, existing slides → transformed into polished presentations
- Multiple export formats: PDF, PPTX, PNG, live web link
- Developer API: programmatic creation supporting 60+ languages and customizable themes
- 250M+ presentations generated; dedicated educator solution

**Why it works:** Gamma eliminates the "blank canvas" problem entirely. Teachers focus on WHAT to teach, not HOW to make it look good. The web-native approach means content is always shareable and up-to-date without re-sending files.

### 6. TeachShare AI — The All-in-One Resource Creation & Sharing Platform
**What they do brilliantly:**
- Transforms teaching ideas into ready-to-use classroom resources (full lessons, worksheets, activities) in under 3 minutes
- Purpose-built for K-12 instruction (not a generic AI tool adapted for education)
- Standards-aligned, differentiated content supporting various grade levels and reading abilities
- Bulk grading capability — grade multiple student submissions efficiently
- Modify assignments for SPED students: adjust complexity, customize language, add supports without redoing the entire lesson
- Collaborative sharing: teachers discover, share, and customize each other's lesson plans across schools and districts
- YouTube video → assessment questions: paste a video link → auto-generates MCQs, open-ended prompts, teacher guide
- Template-based generation: Choose assessment type (Compare & Contrast, Main Idea, Fill-in-Blank) and AI generates content
- Auto-grading + Google Classroom integration
- Scaffolding built into the creation process (breaks complex tasks into supported steps)
- Supports 10+ languages

**Why it works:** TeachShare understands that teachers need BOTH creation AND modification. You don't always start from scratch — sometimes you have existing resources that need adapting for different learners. The collaborative sharing means the best resources get refined collectively across the profession.

### 7. Canvas Teaching AI (IgniteAI) — Embedded AI Agent for Course Management
**What they do brilliantly:**
- AI embedded DIRECTLY into the workflow (not a separate tool — it's WHERE teachers already work)
- **AI Teaching Agent** that automates "low-value" tasks: rubric generation, content alignment, discussion reviews
- **Rubric Generator**: analyses assignment descriptions + learning outcomes + course context → generates clear, customizable criteria in seconds
- **Grading Assistance (SpeedGrader)**: generates suggested scores and feedback for written assignments based on associated rubrics
- **Discussion Insights**: AI reviews discussion posts to surface participation patterns and engagement
- **Translation**: automatic translation for discussions, inbox messages, and announcements
- **Question Authoring Assistance**: helps create quiz questions aligned to content
- **Content alignment checking**: verifies course content matches stated learning outcomes
- Instructor-only visibility: AI features are teacher-facing, not visible to students
- Opt-in approach: features are off by default, teachers choose what to enable
- Integrated with the entire LMS ecosystem (assignments, grades, discussions, quizzes)

**Why it works:** Canvas understood that the BEST AI is INVISIBLE — it lives where teachers already work and handles the tedious parts (rubric formatting, discussion monitoring, alignment checking) so teachers can focus on actual teaching and meaningful feedback. The agent concept is powerful: it doesn't just generate content, it actively manages and reviews existing content.

## PART 2: THE SEND TWIST — YOUR UNIQUE SELLING PROPOSITION

### Why SEND Changes Everything

These platforms are built for mainstream classrooms. They assume:
- Students can read at grade level
- Students can process standard text sizes and layouts
- Students can handle a typical cognitive load
- Students don't need sensory accommodations
- One difficulty level fits a whole class
- Students can work independently through multi-step processes

**Your platform DOESN'T assume any of this.** Every single tool is built knowing that:
- Reading levels vary enormously (pre-reader to functional reader)
- Visual processing needs differ (dyslexia-friendly fonts, high contrast, minimal clutter)
- Cognitive load must be carefully managed (chunking, scaffolding, repetition)
- Sensory needs are real (low-stimulus options, no sudden animations, calm colour palettes)
- Communication methods vary (symbol support, Makaton alignment, visual schedules)
- Attention spans differ (shorter activities, built-in breaks, reward systems)
- Fine motor skills vary (larger click targets, drag-and-drop alternatives, switch access)
- Emotional regulation is part of learning (calming features, celebration without overwhelm)

### TOOL 1: SEND Connected Resource Generator
*Inspired by: EasyClass.ai's Connected OS*

**The Concept:** Teacher enters a topic and student profile → generates a COMPLETE differentiated resource pack, all connected and aligned.

**What it generates from ONE input:**
1. **Differentiated Worksheet** (3-5 levels: symbol-supported → simple text → extended)
2. **Visual Presentation** (Colourful Semantics-coded, Widgit/symbol-supported, sensory-friendly)
3. **Adapted Reading Passage** (multiple reading levels, with comprehension supports built in)
4. **Interactive Quiz** (accessible formats: drag-and-drop, picture-match, switch-accessible)
5. **Visual Schedule/Task Breakdown** (shows the student WHAT they're doing and WHEN)
6. **Communication Board** (topic-specific vocabulary for AAC users)
7. **Teacher/TA Guide** (prompts, scaffolding tips, sensory break reminders)

**SEND-Specific Differentiators:**
- **Learner Profile Selector:** Choose from profiles like "Pre-verbal with symbol support", "Dyslexic reader", "ADHD - short burst activities", "ASD - needs predictability", "PMLD - sensory approach", or create custom profiles
- **Colourful Semantics Integration:** Automatically colour-codes sentences (who/what doing/what/where)
- **Symbol Support Toggle:** Add Widgit/PCS symbols above/below any text
- **Sensory-Friendly Themes:** Resources generated in calm palettes, clean layouts, dyslexia-friendly fonts
- **Chunked Output:** Everything broken into small, manageable steps
- **Now-Next-Then Boards:** Auto-generated visual schedules for each activity
- **Reward Markers:** Built-in celebration points ("You've finished 2 of 4 activities!")

**How it's BETTER than EasyClass.ai:**
- EasyClass generates ONE level. Yours generates MULTIPLE differentiated levels simultaneously
- EasyClass doesn't consider accessibility. Yours has it at the core
- EasyClass doesn't include visual supports, communication boards, or sensory considerations
- Your platform understands that in a SEND classroom, the TA guide is as important as the student resource

### TOOL 3: SEND Interactive Activity Generator
*Inspired by: ToolsEdu.com's Game Makers*

**The Concept:** Generate accessible, interactive learning games tailored to SEND needs — from the same topic/vocabulary input.

**Activity Types Generated:**

#### Accessible Puzzles & Games:
1. **Symbol Word Search** — Instead of just letters, find symbols/pictures in a grid
2. **Adapted Crossword** — Picture clues, symbol clues, or simplified text clues with word banks
3. **Matching Games** — Word-to-symbol, word-to-picture, symbol-to-real photo
4. **Sequencing Activities** — Drag visual steps into correct order (daily routines, story events, science processes)
5. **Odd One Out** — Visual categorisation with pictures (which doesn't belong?)
6. **Bingo Boards** — Generated with symbols/pictures, playable with eye gaze or switch access
7. **Spot the Difference** — Visual attention activities
8. **Barrier Games** — Describing and listening activities with visual supports

#### Interactive Digital Activities:
9. **Drag-and-Drop Sorting** — Categorise items with large click targets
10. **Cause & Effect Games** — Press/click ANYTHING and something happens (for early learners)
11. **Choice-Making Activities** — Select from 2-4 options with symbol/picture support
12. **Fill-in-the-Blank** — With word banks and symbol hints
13. **Colouring/Labelling** — Digital activities that build vocabulary
14. **Sound Matching** — Hear a sound, match to the picture
15. **Emotion Matching** — Match facial expressions to situations/feelings words

**SEND-Specific Differentiators vs ToolsEdu:**
- Every activity has **multiple access methods**: touch, click, eye gaze, switch, keyboard
- **Adjustable complexity**: same game, but simplify from 12 items down to 2-3
- **Sensory reward system**: choose reward type (fireworks vs gentle stars vs simple tick)
- **Timer options**: no timer (removes pressure), visual sand timer, or timed challenge
- **Error handling**: wrong answers get gentle redirect, not harsh "WRONG!" — shows the right answer supportively
- **Print + Digital**: every activity works on screen AND prints beautifully for paper-based learners
- **Attention to cognitive load**: activities never have too much on screen at once
- **Progress without pressure**: completion celebrated, scores optional/hidden from student

### TOOL 4: SEND Visual Learning Studio
*Inspired by: MyLens.ai's Visual Thinking Platform*

**The Concept:** Turn any topic into accessible, interactive visual learning tools — designed for how SEND learners actually process information.

**Visual Types Generated:**

1. **Visual Timetables/Schedules**
   - Input a day's activities → generates Now/Next/Then boards, full day schedules with symbols
   - Printable AND digital interactive versions
   - Countdown timers, transition warnings built in

2. **Topic Mind Maps (Accessible)**
   - Colour-coded branches (not overwhelming — max 4-5 branches)
   - Symbol + text on every node
   - Clickable to expand one section at a time (reduces overwhelm)
   - "Learning Journey" mode: reveals one branch at a time as student progresses

3. **Social Stories as Visual Flowcharts**
   - Input a situation → generates a visual flowchart/comic-strip story
   - "If this happens → I can do this → then this will happen"
   - Calm, clear illustrations at each step
   - Printable for wallets/desks AND digital interactive version

4. **Process/Sequencing Visuals**
   - Science experiments, cooking steps, getting dressed, hand washing
   - Large, clear numbered steps with real photos or symbols
   - Checkboxes to tick off each step
   - "What comes next?" interactive mode

5. **Vocabulary Webs**
   - Central word/concept with connected: picture, symbol, definition, example, related words
   - Multi-sensory: hear it, see it, see it used, connect it
   - Builds over time into a personal dictionary

6. **Emotion/Behaviour Visuals**
   - Zones of Regulation colour-coded visuals
   - "When I feel X, I can try Y" flowcharts
   - Anger/anxiety scales with visual indicators
   - Calming strategy choice boards

7. **Curriculum Topic Summaries**
   - Input: a topic like "The Water Cycle" or "World War 2"
   - Output: simplified, symbol-supported visual summary
   - Key facts presented as visual infographic (not walls of text)
   - Timeline mode for history, cycle mode for science, map mode for geography

**SEND-Specific Differentiators vs MyLens:**
- MyLens assumes users can read and process complex visuals. Yours uses **progressive disclosure** — reveal information gradually
- Symbol support on ALL visuals (not just text)
- **Sensory-friendly colour schemes** — no harsh contrasts or overwhelming busy-ness
- **Interactive pacing** — student controls how much they see at once
- Every visual is a **learning tool**, not just information display (built-in questions, check understanding moments)
- **Print-first design** — many SEND settings still rely heavily on printed visuals
- Visuals align with established SEND frameworks (Colourful Semantics, Zones of Regulation, TEACCH visual structures)

### 11.2 — The single most important finding

**The platform you specifically admired for visuals — MyLens — does NOT use an image model.** It uses an LLM to produce *structured data*, then renders **vector diagrams in code**. Its visuals look "high-level and relevant" precisely *because* they are generated from the actual content structure, not keyword-matched to a stock library.

**You already have this exact architecture** — `PresentationDiagram.tsx` renders SVG flowcharts, Venn diagrams, timelines, cycles, cells, food chains, circuits, etc. from AI-structured data. So the "MyLens-quality, always-relevant" visual experience is achievable **for free, deterministically, with the models you already run.** This should be the backbone of your visual strategy.

### 11.3 — Why your current images can feel "random / half-matching"

Your current photo pipeline is: AI writes **one keyword** → `/api/image-proxy/search` → Pexels/Unsplash returns the **first approximate match**. Two weak links:
1. **One vague keyword** ("ocean") → broad, generic results.
2. **No relevance scoring** → you take whatever the stock API ranks first, which optimises for *popularity/aesthetics*, not *relevance to your slide*.

### 11.4 — The fix: a 4-tier visual engine (all free, relevance-first)

Resolve every visual in this priority order. Higher tiers are more relevant AND cheaper:

```
TIER 1 — STRUCTURED VECTOR (free, always relevant, print-perfect)
  If the content is a process/comparison/timeline/cycle/relationship/data
  → render with your existing SVG diagram engine (the "MyLens approach").
  This should cover the majority of educational visuals.

TIER 2 — CURATED SEND BANK (free, vetted, safe)
  Common curriculum topics + ARASAAC symbols (free, CC-licensed, SEND-standard).
  Deterministic, child-safe, no API call.

TIER 3 — SMART STOCK SEARCH (free, now actually relevant)
  Only when a real-world PHOTO is needed. Two upgrades over today:
   (a) LLM generates 3–5 SPECIFIC queries + a one-line "ideal image" description
       instead of one keyword.
   (b) Re-rank the candidates with CLIP so the BEST-MATCHING photo wins,
       not just the most popular. CLIP runs free on Cloudflare Workers AI
       or in-browser via transformers.js. THIS is what kills "half-matching."

TIER 4 — GENERATIVE (free-ish, last resort, for story illustrations only)
  Only for unique scenes (e.g. a child's storybook page) where no stock/
  vector option exists. Use a free generator (see 11.5). Always safety-gated.
```

### 11.6 — HONEST LIMITATIONS IN YOUR APPROACH

These are the real tensions between "free + high-quality + relevant + child-safe." You asked for honesty:

1. **"Free" + "AI-generated illustrations" partially conflict at scale.** Free image generation exists, but free tiers (Cloudflare 10k neurons/day, Pollinations credits) will throttle if a whole multi-academy trust generates thousands of images a day. Fine for a school; a ceiling for a scaled product. → *Mitigation: lean on Tiers 1–3 (vector + stock) which have no meaningful cap; reserve generation for genuine story-illustration needs and cache aggressively.*

2. **"High quality" generative ≠ free quality.** Gamma's look comes from Imagen-4 / Flux-Kontext-Max — paid, premium. Free FLUX-schnell/klein is good but a visible step down, and **character consistency across story pages (the BuildMyStory effect) is genuinely hard with free models.** → *Mitigation: accept "good, consistent-enough" for v1; use a fixed style prompt + seed to improve consistency.*

3. **Child-safety is a real blocker for free generative endpoints.** Pexels/Unsplash/ARASAAC are **pre-vetted and safe**. Free generative APIs have **weaker moderation** — unacceptable for unsupervised SEND-pupil-facing image creation without a safety filter in front. → *Mitigation: keep generation teacher-initiated/reviewed, never pupil-direct, and add a moderation pass.*

4. **The relevance fix needs CLIP, which is compute.** Re-ranking is what removes "half-matching," but running CLIP in-browser adds bundle weight and load — and **SEND settings often run low-spec devices**. → *Mitigation: run CLIP server-side on Cloudflare Workers AI, not in the pupil's browser.*

5. **Your true USP — SEND symbols (Widgit/PCS/ARASAAC) — is NOT solved by ANY image model.** No amount of generation or stock search produces a consistent AAC symbol set. This must come from a **symbol library (ARASAAC, free + legal)**. Don't let the "AI visuals" excitement distract from the symbol-proxy, which is the actually-differentiating piece. → *Build the ARASAAC symbol-proxy first.*

6. **Client-side API keys (your current localStorage pattern) are fine for text but risky for image-gen keys.** → *Route all image generation through a server proxy (you already have the `image-proxy.ts` pattern to extend), never expose generative keys client-side.*

7. **Determinism vs. novelty trade-off.** Vector + curated tiers are reliable and repeatable (great for SEND consistency, which pupils depend on); generation is novel but unpredictable. For SEND, **predictability is usually a feature, not a limitation** — which conveniently aligns with the cheapest, most relevant approach.

### 11.7 — Bottom line recommendation

- **Make the SVG/vector engine (MyLens-style) your primary "visual" — it's free, always relevant, print-perfect, and you already have it.**
- **Fix stock relevance** with multi-query LLM prompts + server-side CLIP re-ranking (free on Cloudflare). This alone removes most "half-matching."
- **Build the ARASAAC symbol-proxy** — your real, free, legal SEND differentiator.
- **Add generation (Cloudflare FLUX) only for story illustrations**, teacher-reviewed and safety-gated, with caching.
- **Demote the dead Gemini key**; keep Groq primary for text.
- Net result: **high-quality, genuinely relevant, child-safe visuals at $0 infrastructure** — without depending on Gemini, and without the random/half-matching problem.
